# Architecture Clinic Guide

## Purpose

Understand how one real memory or companion system works before deciding how Relata should test it. The clinic is not a product ranking or architecture defense.

## Inputs

- one System Card;
- an optional diagram or artifact trace;
- one known success and one known failure;
- the contributor’s chosen privacy boundary.

## Clinic sequence

1. **System identity and use:** what relationship or work does this system sustain?
2. **Remembered objects:** raw events, summaries, claims, state, identity, intentions, or other forms.
3. **Write path:** what enters automatically, manually, or through reflection/consolidation?
4. **Change path:** correction, supersession, coexistence, expiry, deletion, and uncertainty.
5. **Recall path:** triggers, search, working memory, active context, and response use.
6. **Authority:** how speaker, source, inference, and acceptance are represented.
7. **Surfaces and migration:** what changes across apps, models, instances, or devices?
8. **User control:** inspect, amend, revoke, export, explain.
9. **Known failures:** where continuity breaks or memory becomes intrusive.
10. **Relata pressure:** which proposed operation would be native, emulated, impossible, or misleading?

## Output

- reviewed System Card;
- one row/column contribution to the Architecture Pressure Map;
- a list of protocol assumptions that would distort this system;
- candidate case ideas;
- unresolved questions.

## Rules

- Do not require source code, credentials, or production logs.
- Do not convert “unsupported” into “inferior.”
- Distinguish absent capability from capability hidden behind the proposed boundary.
- Preserve architecture-specific vocabulary before translating it into Relata terms.
