# Relata Research Questions

Each research question names the evidence appropriate to its claim: exact sources, community-grounded incidents, system observations, synthetic cases or controls as needed. A conceptual or source study need not include every evidence route. A polished definition alone does not establish runtime behavior or settle competing interpretations.

## RQ1 — What exactly is being evaluated?

Possible objects include a memory engine, context compiler, complete companion agent, relationship trajectory, mixed-domain memory ecology, repair process, or user-governance surface.

**Evidence needed:** competing decompositions, causal controls, and pilot observations showing which failures can and cannot be attributed at each boundary.

## RQ2 — What is the remembered object?

Candidate objects include raw episodes, ordinary events, people and places, claims, facts, preferences, project state, artifacts and source authority, tasks and blockers, decisions and rationale, milestones, working context, external references, handoff state, unfinished threads, rituals, roles, interpretations, relationship state, future intentions, unresolved tensions, and model or instance identity. Some systems may preserve continuity without exposing a discrete “memory object.”

**Evidence needed:** System Cards plus incidents where materially different architectures handle the same personal, relational, operational, or companion-continuity phenomenon.

## RQ3 — How do authority, perspective, consent, and disagreement work?

Relata must distinguish speaker, subject, recorder, interpreter, accepter, corrector, revoker, and scope holder. It must represent unresolved disagreement without forcing false consensus.

**Evidence needed:** cases involving companion inference, third-party claims, jointly accepted interpretations, corrections, revocations, and contested relational meaning.

## RQ4 — What counts as current state, history, transition, and conditional coexistence?

“Latest wins” is sometimes correct and sometimes destructive. Multiple states can coexist by surface, trigger, role, time, or explicit condition.

**Evidence needed:** counterfactual twin groups where only the transition or condition changes.

## RQ5 — When should relevant memory remain silent?

Retrieval relevance, context eligibility, and response appropriateness are separate questions.

**Evidence needed:** incidents and cases involving sensitive resurfacing, private/public boundaries, unrelated vulnerability, and memory-display behavior.

## RQ6 — How can memory contribution be causally identified?

A good final response can come from the current turn, model priors, system prompt, persona, or memory. A bad response can arise at retention, state change, activation, context construction, or response use.

**Evidence needed:** current-turn-only, no-memory, full-history/full-search, bounded-context, reference-context, and system-native controls; cross-domain ablation and project-isolated versus merged-history comparison where relevant; preserve intermediate artifacts only where the system exposes them.

## RQ7 — What system-under-study boundary compares systems without erasing them?

Write/search operations, evidence recall, rendered context, and complete-agent interaction expose different capabilities and create different distortions.

**Evidence needed:** Architecture Pressure Maps and adapter-distortion reports across materially different systems, including opaque systems.

## RQ8 — Which judgments are reproducible?

Some assertions can be exact. Others require bounded semantic judgment. Some remain culturally or relationally contested.

**Evidence needed:** annotation anchors, blind review, reviewer-group comparison, disagreement analysis, and model-judge audits only after human review contracts stabilize.

## RQ9 — How should real experience inform a synthetic public casebook?

Relata needs lived expertise without turning private relationships into harvested datasets.

**Evidence needed:** per-contribution consent records, abstract Incident Seeds, contributor review of synthetic derivations, data minimization, and an honest withdrawal process.

## RQ10 — What would make Relata useful to builders, researchers and relationship communities?

Research is useful when its evidence supports understanding or action: a clearer concept, an architectural selection, a relational distinction, a system diagnosis, a regression case, a governance repair, or an explicit limit on what can be claimed.

**Evidence needed:** source studies and documented uses of research judgments; pilot postmortems showing whether contributors and maintainers can locate a failure or ambiguity without reconstructing hidden evaluator reasoning. Adoption alone does not demonstrate benefit.

## RQ11 — How do mixed memory domains coexist and route?

How should personal life, shared relationship history, operational projects, and companion identity influence one another without being flattened into a single profile or leaking across roles, surfaces, projects, people, or instances?

**Evidence needed:** multi-project and multi-surface System Cards; cross-domain Incident Seeds; routing and isolation counterfactuals; full-history/full-search and system-native baselines; and cases where the same memory is valid in one role but distracting, private, stale, or misleading in another.

## RQ12 — What do different projects mean by agent memory?

How do memory, persistence, context management, retrieval, state, learning and experience reuse overlap or differ in specific implementations? What from the past persists, how can it affect present behavior, and where does that influence reside? Files, complete histories, external stores, agent-controlled context and learned changes are candidates to examine, not a prescribed taxonomy.

**Evidence needed:** competing definitions tied to exact papers and source paths, native lifecycle descriptions, and counterexamples that change the proposed boundary. The [agent-memory inquiry](research/agent-memory-inquiry.md) begins this work without accepting a definition.

## RQ13 — What are the conditional strengths and limitations of open-source memory architectures?

Which choices trade fidelity, change handling, scope isolation, usefulness, inspectability, portability, cost and maintenance against one another? What does the public implementation supply, and what must the model, harness, application or human supply? Compare the actual version and configuration, distinguishing libraries, full agents, hosted products and historical implementations.

**Evidence needed:** source-reviewed System Cards, inspectable mechanism comparisons and evidence adequate for each outcome claim. A paper or vendor-reported score is not automatically a result for the public repository. Source analysis can identify a design tradeoff without claiming a measured system failure.

## RQ14 — Which conclusions transfer across general and intimate uses?

Which continuity requirements are shared by task-oriented agents, ordinary life and adult long-term intimacy, and which depend on the local situation? Can the same architecture support project authority and shared authorship, scoped correction, unfinished intentions and appropriate silence without treating all contexts as interchangeable?

**Evidence needed:** matched and contrasting use scenarios, explicit local expectations, source mechanisms and bounded observations. Relationship-specific demands must not become hidden universal product requirements; general benchmark success does not establish relational continuity.
