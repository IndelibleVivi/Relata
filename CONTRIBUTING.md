# Contributing to Relata

Relata accepts research, lived-experience, technical, editorial, linguistic, case, and governance contributions. Coding skill is not a prerequisite. The source repository is public. Public-safe code, documentation, source research, and synthetic-case changes may use normal GitHub contributions; intimate lived-experience material, identifying consent records, and restricted system details must not be submitted through public issues or pull requests.

Community contributors participate as co-researchers. They may challenge project terms, constructs, cases, and boundaries rather than merely supply examples to a fixed benchmark.

## Contribution routes

### Evidence Card

A close read or source-level audit tied to an exact public source and version. Use the [Evidence Card template](research/evidence-card-template.md).

### Incident Seed

An abstract account of a relational-memory failure or moment of continuity. Raw chat is not required. Use the [Chinese](community/incident-seed-template.zh-CN.md) or [English](community/incident-seed-template.md) template.

### System Card

A system-native description of an actual memory or companion architecture, including what is observable, opaque, adapter-emulated, unsupported, or unknown. Use the [Chinese](systems/system-card-template.zh-CN.md) or [English](systems/system-card-template.md) template.

### Case contribution

An adult synthetic microcase with a bounded construct, counterfactual twin, controls, probe evidence contract, ambiguity analysis, and privacy declaration. Use the [Case Card template](case-lab/case-card-template.md).

### Review or governance contribution

A critique of a construct, case, reviewer contract, architecture boundary, term, consent route, attribution rule, or publication boundary. Reviews may disagree with project assumptions and should preserve the evidence for that disagreement.

## Contribution process during R0

1. Choose permissions and attribution per contribution; do not infer them from group membership or prior participation.
2. Store any identifying consent record outside the public repository using `community/contribution-consent-record-template.md`.
3. Share the smallest artifact that carries the insight.
4. Separate observed or sourced material from interpretation.
5. Identify the Relata question, distinction, assumption, case, or decision affected.
6. Receive review from at least one person outside the original authoring context.
7. Keep unresolved disagreement visible.
8. Promote material into accepted project authority only through the relevant Evidence Card, case review, assumption update, or decision record.

## Privacy

Do not include:

- credentials, tokens, private infrastructure addresses, or private repository links;
- raw or directly identifying real-chat material;
- identifying consent records;
- another contributor’s system or relationship details without permission;
- private system configuration supplied under restricted visibility.

A contributor may keep all source material local and submit only an abstract Incident Seed or a public-safe system summary.

Public visibility does not replace per-contribution permission, and this repository does not currently provide a restricted submission route.

## Attribution and review

Contributors choose named, pseudonymous, collective, or anonymous attribution per artifact. Public provenance should preserve authorship and derivation without exposing private source material. Silence is not approval when pre-publication review was promised.

See [consent modes](community/consent-levels.md), [participation principles](community/participation-principles.md), and [attribution and withdrawal](governance/attribution-and-withdrawal.md).

## Review standard

A useful contribution states:

- what exact object was observed or sourced;
- what is inferred;
- what remains unknown or opaque;
- which architecture or relationship assumptions shape the conclusion;
- what project decision, construct, or case changes because of it.
