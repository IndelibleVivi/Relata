# Attribution and Withdrawal

## Attribution options

A contributor may choose:

- legal or public name;
- stable pseudonym;
- collective/community attribution;
- anonymous contribution;
- no public contribution.

Attribution choices apply per artifact and may differ across Incident Seeds, System Cards, cases, and reviews.

## Provenance

Public artifacts should preserve authorship and derivation history without exposing private source material. Editors must not erase the origin of a public contribution or present community knowledge as solely maintainer-authored.

## Pre-publication review

When a synthetic case derives under Consent Mode 3, or a public artifact is proposed under Mode 4, the contributor receives the exact material promised for pre-publication review. Silence is not treated as approval unless that mechanism was explicitly and knowingly agreed in advance.

## Withdrawal before release

Before public release, the maintained project removes the contribution and any unpublished derivation that depends on it, unless the contributor agrees to a more limited change.

## Withdrawal after release

After release, maintainers can:

- remove the artifact from future maintained releases;
- mark it withdrawn or deprecated in project history;
- stop using it in active evaluation;
- remove identity links where feasible;
- publish a correction to attribution or provenance.

Maintainers cannot promise deletion from third-party copies, forks, archives, citations, or previously distributed release bundles.

## Derived work review

When one contribution materially shapes a synthetic case, editors should record that derivation relationship in the restricted workspace and honor the agreed attribution. Aggregate influence that cannot be traced to one contribution should be described honestly as collective community input.

Identifying attribution and consent records stay outside the public repository. Public artifacts use contribution IDs and only the attribution fields authorized for publication.
